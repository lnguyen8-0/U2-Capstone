1. Information Security Policy
1.1 Purpose
Northstar Solutions protects company information, customer data, employee information, and technology assets from unauthorized access, misuse, alteration, disclosure, or destruction.
1.2 Scope
This policy applies to:
•	All employees, contractors, interns, and temporary staff
•	Company-owned and approved personal devices used for business
•	Company applications, cloud platforms, networks, and databases
•	Information created, received, processed, or stored on behalf of Northstar Solutions
1.3 Information Classification
Company information must be classified according to its sensitivity:
Public
Information approved for public release, such as published marketing materials and job postings.
Internal
Information intended for employees and approved contractors, such as internal procedures and team documentation.
Confidential
Information that could harm the company or its customers if improperly disclosed, such as customer contracts, pricing, forecasts, and employee records.
Restricted
Highly sensitive information requiring enhanced protection, such as passwords, encryption keys, payment information, personal identification information, and security incident details.
Employees must not share Confidential or Restricted information through unapproved applications or personal email accounts.
1.4 Account and Password Security
All users must:
•	Use a unique password for each company system
•	Use multi-factor authentication where available
•	Never share passwords, authentication codes, or security tokens
•	Lock their screen when leaving a device unattended
•	Report suspected credential compromise immediately
•	Use only company-approved password managers for storing business credentials
Passwords must not be written on visible notes or stored in unencrypted documents.
1.5 Device and Software Security
Company devices must:
•	Use supported operating systems
•	Have automatic security updates enabled
•	Use approved endpoint protection
•	Be protected by a screen lock
•	Connect to company systems only through approved methods
•	Be reported promptly if lost, stolen, or compromised
Employees may not install unapproved software that could introduce security, licensing, or privacy risks.
1.6 Data Handling
Employees must use the minimum amount of information necessary to perform their duties. Confidential and Restricted data should be:
•	Stored only in approved systems
•	Shared only with authorized recipients
•	Encrypted when transmitted externally
•	Deleted or archived according to retention requirements
•	Reviewed for accuracy before being shared or used for decisions
Sensitive customer information must not be copied into public artificial intelligence tools or other unapproved external services.
1.7 Security Incident Reporting
A security incident may include:
•	A lost or stolen device
•	A phishing email or suspicious attachment
•	Accidental disclosure of confidential information
•	Unauthorized access
•	Malware or ransomware activity
•	Misuse of company systems
Employees must report suspected incidents to the Service Desk or Security Team as soon as possible. Reports should include what happened, when it happened, which systems or information may be affected, and any actions already taken.
Employees should not investigate beyond their training or delete evidence related to a suspected incident.
1.8 Remote Work and Public Networks
When working remotely, employees must:
•	Use secure and private network connections where possible
•	Avoid discussing confidential matters in public locations
•	Use privacy screens when appropriate
•	Store documents only in approved company systems
•	Avoid using public computers for company work
•	Follow company requirements for virtual private network access
1.9 Policy Violations
Violations may result in additional training, access restrictions, disciplinary action, or termination of employment, depending on the nature and severity of the violation.
2. Software Development and Code Review Policy
2.1 Purpose
This policy establishes a consistent process for developing, reviewing, testing, and releasing software at Northstar Solutions.
2.2 Scope
The policy applies to:
•	Application code
•	Infrastructure and deployment scripts
•	Database changes
•	Configuration files
•	Automation scripts
•	Documentation that affects system operation
2.3 Development Branches
Developers should create a separate branch for each feature, defect correction, or technical improvement. Branch names should clearly describe the work, such as:
•	feature/customer-search
•	bugfix/login-timeout
•	chore/update-dependencies
Direct commits to the production branch are not permitted except through an approved emergency process.
2.4 Pull Requests
All material code changes must be submitted through a pull request. A pull request should include:
•	A concise summary of the change
•	The business or technical reason for the change
•	Testing performed
•	Known limitations or risks
•	Related work item or issue number, when available
•	Screenshots or examples for user-facing changes
Pull requests should be small enough to review effectively. Large changes should be divided into logical increments when practical.
2.5 Review Requirements
At least one qualified reviewer must approve a standard change before it is merged. Changes affecting authentication, authorization, payment processing, customer data, or production infrastructure require review by an additional subject-matter expert.
Reviewers should evaluate:
•	Correctness and completeness
•	Security and privacy implications
•	Error handling
•	Performance considerations
•	Maintainability and readability
•	Test coverage
•	Backward compatibility
•	Logging and monitoring requirements
•	Documentation impacts
Approval means the reviewer has examined the change against the applicable standards. It does not transfer responsibility away from the author.
2.6 Testing
Developers must test changes before requesting approval. Testing may include:
•	Unit tests
•	Integration tests
•	Regression tests
•	Security tests
•	Performance tests
•	Manual user acceptance checks
Tests should cover both expected behavior and relevant failure conditions. A change that causes a critical test failure must not be merged until the failure is resolved or explicitly approved by the responsible engineering manager.
2.7 Dependency Management
New third-party dependencies require review for:
•	Security vulnerabilities
•	License compatibility
•	Maintenance activity
•	Data-handling behavior
•	Compatibility with the existing technology stack
Dependencies should be kept current according to the team’s maintenance schedule.
2.8 Emergency Changes
Emergency changes may follow an expedited review process when delay could create significant business or customer impact. The change must still be documented, tested to the extent practical, reviewed by an authorized person, and followed by a retrospective review.
2.9 Release Documentation
Each production release should record:
•	Release date
•	Included changes
•	Responsible team
•	Deployment status
•	Rollback or recovery considerations
•	Known issues
•	Validation results
3. Customer Complaint Handling Policy
3.1 Purpose
Northstar Solutions responds to customer complaints consistently, respectfully, and within defined service expectations. Complaints provide an opportunity to identify service gaps and improve customer outcomes.
3.2 Scope
This policy applies to complaints received through:
•	Email
•	Telephone
•	Support portals
•	Account teams
•	Social media channels
•	Surveys
•	Executive escalations
3.3 Complaint Categories
Complaints should be categorized as:
Service Issue
The customer reports poor service, delay, communication problems, or an unresolved request.
Product Issue
The customer reports a defect, outage, usability problem, or missing capability.
Billing Issue
The customer questions an invoice, charge, credit, renewal, or payment.
Security or Privacy Concern
The customer reports a potential data exposure, unauthorized access, or privacy concern.
High-Impact Escalation
The issue involves significant business disruption, repeated failure, executive attention, regulatory sensitivity, or potential reputational harm.
3.4 Intake and Acknowledgment
The employee receiving a complaint must:
1.	Listen without interrupting.
2.	Record the customer’s concern accurately.
3.	Confirm the desired outcome.
4.	Assign the appropriate category and priority.
5.	Provide an acknowledgment and next step.
6.	Route the issue to the responsible team when necessary.
Employees must not make promises about refunds, credits, delivery dates, or technical resolutions unless authorized to do so.
3.5 Response Targets
The following internal targets should guide complaint handling:
•	Standard complaint: acknowledge within one business day
•	High-priority complaint: acknowledge within four business hours
•	Security or privacy concern: route immediately to the Security or Privacy Team
•	Executive escalation: notify the account owner and service manager the same business day
These are operational targets and may vary based on severity, information availability, and customer circumstances.
3.6 Investigation
The responsible owner should review:
•	Customer history
•	Previous communications
•	Service records
•	Relevant system logs
•	Contract or order information
•	Product or operational evidence
•	Impact on the customer
The investigation should distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions. If information is unavailable, the owner should state what remains unverified.
3.7 Resolution and Communication
Customer communications should:
•	Acknowledge the customer’s concern
•	Explain what was confirmed
•	Avoid unsupported conclusions
•	Describe corrective actions
•	Provide a realistic next update
•	Identify the responsible contact
•	Document any unresolved items
Employees should not blame another team or speculate about root cause before the investigation is complete.
3.8 Escalation
A complaint should be escalated when:
•	The customer reports a material business impact
•	The issue remains unresolved after the initial response
•	Similar complaints are increasing
•	A security, privacy, or safety concern is present
•	The customer requests executive involvement
•	The issue may affect multiple customers
3.9 Root Cause and Continuous Improvement
For recurring or high-impact complaints, the responsible team should document:
•	Root cause or contributing factors
•	Customer and business impact
•	Immediate containment
•	Permanent corrective action
•	Action owner
•	Target completion date
•	Method for confirming effectiveness
Complaint trends should be reviewed periodically to identify process, product, training, or communication improvements.
4. Employee Satisfaction and Workplace Experience Policy
4.1 Purpose
Northstar Solutions seeks to create a respectful, inclusive, productive, and sustainable workplace. Employee feedback is used to identify workplace strengths and improvement opportunities.
4.2 Scope
This policy applies to all employees and people managers.
4.3 Feedback Channels
Employees may provide feedback through:
•	Employee engagement surveys
•	Manager one-on-ones
•	Team retrospectives
•	Anonymous feedback channels
•	Human Resources consultations
•	Exit interviews
•	Employee resource or interest groups
Employees are encouraged to provide specific, constructive feedback supported by examples.
4.4 Survey Administration
Employee surveys should be designed to protect confidentiality and encourage honest participation. Results should be reported in aggregate where group size or response volume could otherwise make individuals identifiable.
Survey questions may address:
•	Role clarity
•	Manager effectiveness
•	Workload
•	Career development
•	Recognition
•	Collaboration
•	Psychological safety
•	Tools and processes
•	Intent to remain with the company
4.5 Manager Responsibilities
Managers are expected to:
•	Hold regular one-on-one conversations
•	Clarify goals and priorities
•	Provide timely feedback
•	Recognize contributions
•	Address workload concerns
•	Support professional development
•	Escalate unresolved workplace concerns
•	Follow up on agreed actions
Managers should not retaliate against employees for raising concerns in good faith.
4.6 Action Planning
After an engagement survey or significant feedback exercise, leaders should:
1.	Review the results with the team.
2.	Identify the most important themes.
3.	Select a limited number of practical actions.
4.	Assign owners and target dates.
5.	Communicate progress regularly.
6.	Reassess whether the actions improved the underlying issue.
Action plans should address both immediate improvements and longer-term structural causes.
4.7 Workload and Well-Being
Teams should monitor workload, deadlines, staffing, and recurring after-hours work. Employees should raise capacity concerns early. Managers should adjust priorities, sequencing, or staffing where feasible rather than relying on sustained overwork.
4.8 Professional Development
Employees should have opportunities to discuss:
•	Skills needed for their current role
•	Career interests
•	Training or learning opportunities
•	Mentorship
•	Stretch assignments
•	Performance goals
Development commitments should be revisited during regular check-ins.
4.9 Respectful Workplace
Northstar Solutions does not tolerate harassment, intimidation, retaliation, or disrespectful conduct. Concerns should be reported through a manager, Human Resources, Ethics, or another approved reporting channel.
Reports should be handled appropriately and shared only with people who need the information to address the concern.
5. Customer Success Strategy
5.1 Purpose
The Customer Success Strategy defines how Northstar Solutions helps customers achieve measurable value, adopt products effectively, and maintain strong long-term relationships.
5.2 Customer Success Principles
Customer success teams should:
•	Understand the customer’s business objectives
•	Connect product capabilities to measurable outcomes
•	Identify risks early
•	Communicate proactively
•	Coordinate across sales, support, product, and delivery teams
•	Use evidence rather than assumptions
•	Treat customer feedback as an input to improvement
5.3 Customer Segmentation
Customers may be grouped according to:
•	Business size
•	Industry
•	Product usage
•	Revenue contribution
•	Implementation complexity
•	Support requirements
•	Strategic importance
•	Expansion potential
Segmentation should inform service models but should not reduce the quality or respect provided to any customer.
5.4 Onboarding
A standard onboarding plan should include:
•	Customer objectives
•	Key stakeholders
•	Implementation milestones
•	Required customer inputs
•	Training plan
•	Success measures
•	Risks and dependencies
•	First-value target
•	Transition to ongoing support
The customer success owner should confirm that the customer understands responsibilities, milestones, and escalation paths.
5.5 Success Plans
Each strategic or high-complexity customer should have a success plan containing:
•	Customer business priorities
•	Desired outcomes
•	Current baseline
•	Measures of progress
•	Key activities
•	Owners
•	Risks
•	Review cadence
•	Next scheduled milestone
Success plans should be reviewed regularly and updated when customer priorities change.
5.6 Health Indicators
Customer health may be assessed using indicators such as:
•	Product adoption
•	Usage frequency
•	Feature utilization
•	Support volume
•	Complaint trends
•	Renewal timing
•	Stakeholder engagement
•	Achievement of stated outcomes
•	Payment or billing issues
•	Executive relationship strength
Health scores are indicators, not definitive conclusions. Teams should validate the underlying evidence before taking major action.
5.7 Risk Management
A customer risk should be recorded when there is evidence of:
•	Declining usage
•	Unresolved critical issues
•	Missed implementation milestones
•	Low stakeholder engagement
•	Repeated complaints
•	Unclear value realization
•	Competitive displacement risk
•	Organizational change at the customer
Each material risk should have an owner, mitigation plan, and review date.
5.8 Customer Reviews
Business reviews should focus on:
•	Progress against customer objectives
•	Outcomes achieved
•	Usage and adoption trends
•	Open issues
•	Risks and mitigations
•	Upcoming priorities
•	Decisions or support required
Reviews should be prepared using accurate, current information and should clearly distinguish facts, estimates, and recommendations.
5.9 Feedback to Product and Operations
Customer feedback should be recorded with:
•	Customer need or problem
•	Frequency or scale of the issue
•	Business impact
•	Affected customer segment
•	Current workaround
•	Supporting examples
•	Recommended priority, if appropriate
Product requests should not be represented as committed roadmap items unless formally approved.
6. Records Retention and Document Management
6.1 Purpose
Northstar Solutions maintains business records so that information is reliable, accessible, protected, and appropriately disposed of when no longer needed.
6.2 Document Standards
Business documents should include, where relevant:
•	Document title
•	Owner
•	Version
•	Effective date
•	Review date
•	Status
•	Classification
•	Approval record
6.3 Storage
Employees must store business records in approved repositories. Local device storage should be used only when necessary and should not be the sole location for important business information.
6.4 Version Control
When a document is updated, the owner should record the change history. Obsolete versions should be clearly marked or removed from active-use locations to reduce the risk of relying on outdated information.
6.5 Disposal
Records should be securely deleted or destroyed according to applicable retention requirements. Employees must not delete records that are subject to an investigation, dispute, audit, or preservation request.
7. Policy Governance
7.1 Policy Ownership
The document owner is responsible for:
•	Maintaining accurate policy content
•	Coordinating reviews
•	Tracking approvals
•	Communicating material changes
•	Retiring obsolete versions
7.2 Exceptions
Exceptions should be documented with:
•	Policy requirement being excepted
•	Business reason
•	Scope and duration
•	Risk assessment
•	Compensating controls
•	Approving authority
•	Review or expiration date
An exception does not permanently change the underlying policy.
7.3 Review Schedule
This document should be reviewed at least annually and sooner when there are material changes to:
•	Business operations
•	Technology
•	Customers
•	Applicable requirements
•	Organizational responsibilities
•	Security or privacy risks
